# Procedural stellar activity

The optional `StellarEmitter::activity` model adds an opaque photosphere and an
optically thin, raymarched atmosphere. It is a procedural rendering model guided
by solar observations. The four prescribed bipolar regions are not a simulated
magnetic field, and no magnetohydrodynamics (MHD), fusion or flare energy budget
is solved. The separate game owns inspection presets, cameras and time controls.

## References and their consequences

| Primary reference | Observation used | Rendering rule |
| --- | --- | --- |

| [NASA solar photosphere](https://solarscience.msfc.nasa.gov/feature1.shtml) | Granules are about 1,000 km across and evolve over minutes. | Default diameter 1e6 m and representative evolution time 600 s; filter unresolved granules instead of making them enormous at whole-disk distances. |
| [ESA Solar Orbiter corona movie](https://www.esa.int/ESA_Multimedia/Videos/2024/04/The_Sun_s_fluffy_corona_in_exquisite_detail/(lang)/en) | Magnetic arches, moss, spicules and cool dense material within a much hotter atmosphere. | Arches share photospheric footpoints with active regions; moving concentrations follow those arches. | [NASA explanation of solar wavebands](https://svs.gsfc.nasa.gov/vis/a010000/a013800/a013859/script_31299_00.html) | Broadband sunlight and false-colour ultraviolet observations are different views. | Visible continuum and EUV diagnostic are explicit observer modes; diagnostic colour never enters object illumination. |

These links are research references, not bundled image assets. All sandbox
captures are generated by the engine; no reference photographs are redistributed.

## Scene contract

```cpp
scene.stars[0].activity.enabled = true;
scene.timeSeconds = elapsedSimulationSeconds; // caller-owned, deterministic
scene.view = one::StellarView::Visible;
renderer.setStellarScene(scene);
```

Activity is disabled by default. Its validation covers finite values, supported
parameter ranges and a ray budget of 16–192 samples. Use approximately solar
parameters: the procedural atmosphere is not a general stellar classification
model. `seed` fixes the four active regions and convection realization. Motion
uses a latitude-dependent rotation rate and smoothly moving cellular features;
there is no frame-number-dependent randomness. The default rotation is 25.38 days.

The surface is ray/sphere intersected, never made transparent by a noise threshold.
Temperature perturbations modulate the same three Planck bands used by the
emitter. The normalized linear limb law is
`(1-u+u*mu)/(1-u/3)`, with default u=0.6. This law also weights the sixteen
finite-disk illumination samples. Convection and spots alter the displayed local
surface; physical illumination retains the mean blackbody emitter plus the limb
law. This is not local energy-conserving transport from each animated feature.

The atmosphere is evaluated in stellar-radius coordinates. A quiet radial
profile `n_e = n_base * r^-6`, default n_base=4e14 m^-3, is enhanced by Gaussian
arched tubes and a near-surface spicule layer. This is a chosen prototype profile,
not a fitted coronal inversion. The finite outer boundary smoothly tapers away.
Gaussian tube density and squared-density emission measures are independently
filtered over the pixel footprint and ray segment. This reduces disappearance
of narrow tubes when the camera or sample positions move. The thin limb layer
gets a reserved fraction of the ray budget.

Transfer uses front-to-back emission/absorption integration with exponential
segment transmittance. Opaque photospheres and registered spherical bodies clip
the atmosphere. The visible scattering scale uses n_base*sigma_T*R, photospheric
radiance, apparent solar solid angle and the normalized Thomson phase. The phase
uses the central incident direction rather than a full finite-disk angular
integral. A small illustrative red cool-plasma emission term is included; it is
not a calibrated hydrogen-line or non-LTE radiation calculation.

## Observer modes and limits

- **Visible:** Planck-band photosphere, limb darkening and faint scattered corona.
  Normal surface exposure should not reveal a huge bright orange atmosphere.
- **EUV diagnostic:** orange false-colour surface network and density-squared hot
  plasma emission, with cool-material absorption. Intensities, spectral response
  and opacity are illustrative. This is not a calibrated EUI/AIA passband or a
  prediction from ion populations and temperature-dependent line emissivities.
- **Coronagraph:** visible transfer with a central instrument mask at 1.025 times
  the apparent photospheric radius. Increased camera exposure reveals the corona;
  it does not add stellar power. The inner corona hidden by the mask is omitted.

The resolved disk uses activity; subpixel stars retain mean filtered emission.
There is no stellar wind simulation, dynamic magnetic reconnection, realistic
flare/CME evolution, atmospheric planet scattering, arbitrary mesh occultation
or light-travel delay. Other volumetric smoke keeps its existing lighting model.
The raymarch is bounded and runs only within the projected atmosphere. It still
costs more when a star fills the screen; the sandbox exposes its sample budget.

CPU checks cover flux invariance, limb/phase normalization, anchored region
bases, radial density and invalid inputs. GPU checks cover time evolution,
off-limb plasma, foreground clipping, zero-power darkness and observer-colour
isolation in addition to the original stellar regressions. Native inspection
captures and measured runtime results belong in the separate game's validation
record.
